# Original Visual Direction: Industrial Frontier Tactics

## Intent

Create a distinct tactical-industrial science-fantasy presentation suitable
for a compact survival shooter. The design should feel like a field operation
conducted through rugged equipment, not a copy of any existing game's world or
interface.

## Visual vocabulary

- **Environment:** extraction yards, relay stations, abandoned workshops,
  storm barriers, mineral-processing ruins, temporary field bases.
- **Materials:** dark steel, painted panels, canvas, ceramic armor, worn rubber,
  glass displays, hazard tape, dust, and localized emissive elements.
- **Palette:** charcoal and desaturated blue-gray foundations; cyan for neutral
  information; amber for caution; red-orange for immediate danger; pale green
  for recovery. Gameplay contrast takes priority over atmosphere.
- **Shapes:** sturdy rectangles, clipped corners, modular plates, cable runs,
  asymmetric equipment, and simple readable silhouettes.
- **UI:** field-terminal panels, compact labels, numeric hierarchy, restrained
  motion, clear focus states, and icon-plus-text communication.

## Originality boundaries

- Do not use names, faction motifs, logos, portrait compositions, operator
  costumes, animal traits, UI replicas, typography, icons, or terminology from
  Arknights or another commercial property.
- Do not trace screenshots or recreate a recognizable screen layout.
- Build characters from this project's own setting, role, silhouette, palette,
  and equipment briefs. Record asset sources and licenses.
- Inspiration is limited to broad genre qualities: tactical clarity,
  industrial wear, ensemble crews, and science-fantasy field operations.

## Gameplay readability rules

- Enemy, pickup, friendly projectile, hostile hazard, and interactable colors
  must remain distinguishable in grayscale/value as well as hue.
- Telegraphs use shape, timing, and motion in addition to color.
- XP, coins, and temporary buffs must have different silhouettes.
- Upgrade rarity may change border treatment but never hide effect text.
- Screen shake, flashes, and audio layers require intensity controls later.

## Near-term asset policy

Phases 9-13 use simple original placeholders and code-native UI where possible.
Phase 14 replaces them systematically after dimensions, states, and ownership
are stable. New bitmap generation or commissioned art must follow written asset
briefs derived from this document.
